# Changelog

All notable changes are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).
The reader has its own version numbers (SemVer); a release `vX.Y.Z` is the reader version `X.Y.Z`.
Each entry names the versions of the parser (OSLMeta), the flight log module (OSLFlight) and the OpenSkyLab metadata specification it checks against.

## [1.5.0] - 2026-11-25

Parser OSLMeta 1.1.0, flight log module OSLFlight 0.4.0, checks against the OpenSkyLab metadata specification v0.3.

- The "Why it matters" paragraph under the title is folded behind a button with a light-bulb icon; it opens on click (native `<details>`, no script, keyboard accessible). The header is shorter.
- The four cards under the title ("What sets this reader apart") sit in one row, two by two or one column, never three plus a lone fourth; the links under the tool header do not break inside.
- Thermal images: the measurement parameters set in the camera are read (layouts as in ExifTool; checked against ExifTool 12.76 on DJI M3T and senseFly Duet T images).
  - DJI R-JPEG, APP4: ThermalParams2 (M3T and others: emissivity, object distance, relative humidity, reflected and ambient temperature, after an optional 32-byte prefix) and ThermalParams3; ThermalParams of the ZH20T is shown but not used, because the scale of its values is not documented. APP7 `DJI-DBG` gives the SensorID.
  - FLIR FFF in APP1 (for example the senseFly Duet T): the chunks are joined and the CameraInfo and RawData records are read: emissivity, object distance, reflected, atmospheric and IR window temperature, IR window transmission, humidity, the Planck constants R1, B, F, O, R2, the atmospheric transmission coefficients, the camera temperature range and the size and type of the raw thermal image.
  - The check fills `thermal.capture_measurement_params` (marked as camera settings: the ambient temperature and humidity set in the camera are not copied to `environment.*`), `reference.emissivity_setting` (the camera value, to be replaced if the reference instrument used another), `thermal.image_encoding`, `thermal.planck_constants` (FLIR) and `thermal.spectral_range` (XMP Camera band and FWHM).
  - A plausibility check flags emissivity outside 0–1 or equal to 1 (brightness temperature), a DJI distance outside 1–25 m and an object distance far from the laser rangefinder or the height above take-off.
  - For DJI R-JPEG the camera and FPA temperature is reported as not recorded by the camera, instead of asking for it.
  - Temperatures are still not computed: that needs the vendor SDK (for DJI the DJI Thermal SDK, as in ThermoTIFF).
- DJI laser rangefinder (XMP `LRFStatus`, `LRFTargetDistance`): a measured distance fills `environment.sensor_target_distance`; status Normal with distance 0 means no measurement, and the height above take-off is used as before, with a note.
- ThermoTIFF (J. Rous, MIT), which converts DJI R-JPEGs to float32 TIFF in degrees Celsius with the DJI Thermal SDK:
  - its TIFFs are recognised (Software, ImageDescription, GDAL_METADATA UNITTYPE degC) and fill `processing.thermal_settings`, `processing.atmospheric_model` and `processing.software`;
  - its `conversion_log.csv` and `processing_record.json` are read as a processing record (one workflow when both are loaded), with ThermoTIFF, the DJI Thermal SDK API and ExifTool as software agents in `provenance.json` and the conversion as an activity that used the loaded R-JPEGs and generated the loaded TIFFs;
  - folder paths in these files are not copied.
  - Checked on a real ThermoTIFF 0.1.0 run (three DJI M3T R-JPEGs, DJI Thermal SDK API 0x13, humidity and emissivity set by the user): the TIFFs and `conversion_log.csv` read as described; `processing_record.json` only on synthetic files built from the source.
  - `conversion_log.csv` now also gives the temperature range of the images. A minimum or maximum exactly at a DJI measuring range limit (−40, −20, 150, 500 or 550 °C) is flagged as clipped: the SDK clips the values there (in the checked run, sky below −20 °C on an oblique M3T image).
- Flight logs: the state of the flight at each exposure, reported in one check row ("Flight state at the exposures") and in new columns of the exposures CSV (`position`, `satellites`, `hdop`, `eph_m`, `vibration_m_s2`, `flight_mode`).
  - GNSS quality from the record nearest in time (within 2 s): satellites, HDOP (ArduPilot GPS, MAVLink GPS_RAW_INT) or eph (PX4 ULog), fix type. Exposures with a fix below 3D are flagged.
  - Vibration, ArduPilot only (DataFlash VIBE, MAVLink VIBRATION): the largest axis within ±0.5 s and the rise of the clipping counters during imaging, rated with the limits in the ArduPilot documentation (30 and 60 m/s²). These limits concern flight control, so they are only indicative for image blur. PX4 is not rated: its documentation gives no limit.
  - Flight mode in force at each exposure (ArduPilot MODE with Copter, Plane and Rover mode names; PX4 `vehicle_status.nav_state`; MAVLink HEARTBEAT custom mode). Exposures outside the automatic mission are flagged.
  - Autopilot errors and warnings between the first and last exposure: ArduPilot ERR (subsystem names from AP_Logger) and MSG lines about failsafe, crash, glitch, error or loss; ULog logged messages and MAVLink STATUSTEXT of severity warning or worse.
  - An exposure with a time but no position (MAVLink CAMERA_TRIGGER, PX4 camera_capture without a fix) gets a position interpolated linearly from the trajectory, when the two points around it are at most 2 s apart. It is marked with "≈" in the table, as `interpolated` in the CSV and as an estimate (`nInterp`) in `metadata.json` and `provenance.json`.
- A CSV whose coordinate columns have unknown names, but whose first data row holds values that look like degrees, is listed with its columns. The user assigns latitude and longitude (and optionally time, height and image name) in the Flight tab; the file is read again with that assignment, which is recorded as manual.
- Checked on synthetic ArduPilot, MAVLink and ULog files built from the message definitions (ArduPilot LogStructure and mode.h, MAVLink common.xml, PX4 VehicleStatus.msg). The messages are also read correctly from public field logs (ArduCopter 4.6.3 .bin and .tlog, ArduPlane 4.4.4 .bin, PX4 1.16.2 ULog): GPS satellites and HDOP, VIBE, VIBRATION, MODE, HEARTBEAT modes, STATUSTEXT, `nav_state` and logged messages. Three public PX4 mapping flights with `camera_capture` (PX4 1.9 and 1.15, Flight Review, CC-BY; 20, 75 and 296 exposures) give the same exposure counts as the files report, the GNSS quality and the mode at each exposure, and in one flight 40 exposures taken in POSCTL instead of the mission; their error-level messages (low battery) come after the last exposure and are correctly left out. ArduPilot ERR and CAM/TRIG are not yet checked on field logs: no public log with them under a redistributable licence was found.
- A message repeated in the imaging window (a field tlog repeats "Arming Checks Disabled") is listed once with its count.
- Checked on internal WingtraOne field data (WingtraPilot 2.20.3, WingtraHub 2.20.2; Sony a6100 RGB and MicaSense RedEdge-P flights, logs before and after WingtraHub). Fixes found there:
  - ULog, Wingtra variant: logged strings (`C`) carry a one-byte tag, so their text lost its first character and their time was wrong; they are now read whole.
  - ULog: the receiver's UTC time is used only from GNSS records with a fix. A log without a fix gave the year 2011; it now has no UTC time and says so.
  - ULog with an image count in the header but no `camera_capture` messages (the reduced ULog that WingtraHub keeps next to the geotags) is noted; the exposures come from the geotaglog, the JSON or the geotags.csv.
  - Two logs of the same kind that name entirely different images (the RGB and the multispectral `geotags.csv` of one day) are treated as different flights instead of an exposure count mismatch.
  - JPEG: an MPF image list of up to four images (stored as a value array) was not read, so the preview after EOI was reported as an unknown trailer. An MPF preview after zero padding (WingtraHub rewrites APP1 of Sony JPEGs) is recognised as well.
  - JPEG: a `DateTimeOriginal` more than a day away from `CreateDate` is flagged as rewritten after capture (WingtraHub rewrites the time of Sony photos and keeps `CreateDate` from an unset camera clock).
  - The camera clock offset is computed in whole seconds when the reference time has no fraction (`GPSTimeStamp` rewritten without decimals); before, the sub-second part of `DateTimeOriginal` showed as an offset of +0.4 s.
  - MicaSense `httplog.dat` is listed with the other undocumented SD card files.
  - ULog straight from the WingtraOne (not reduced by WingtraHub): it has no `camera_capture`; the exposures now come from `camera_trigger_wingtra` (trigger time in UTC), without positions, because `geotag_dataman_item` is about 0.2 s later and its height is not ellipsoidal. Wingtra numbers its flight modes differently from PX4, so `nav_state` is shown as a number instead of a PX4 name.
  - Messages at the exposures are grouped by text with the numbers left out, errors first: a Wingtra log repeats a few routine payload messages thousands of times.
  - Two logs whose exposures lie more than a minute apart (the RGB and the multispectral flight of one day) are treated as different flights.
  - Septentrio SBF from a rover that logged the camera events without a solution at them (`ExtEvent` only) no longer reports their solution quality as poor; the quality check needs a solution at the event.
  - MicaSense RedEdge-P firmware 1.3.x `gpslog.dat` and `paramlog.dat` added to the checked versions (their epochs, fixes and accuracy agree with each other). Its `paramlog.dat` counts four captures more than the triggers: three before the first trigger and one after the last.
- Wingtra LiDAR (firmware 5.4, checked on an internal `service.log` and `ppk.pcmp`): `service.log` now also gives each `.data` recording with its start time and checksum, the stop time, the full receiver model, the secondary antenna offset, the scanner's azimuth field of view, the navigation solver states, the last LED states, the IMU error model, the IMU latency against GNSS, the clock steering from GNSS (initial step, corrections, frequency), the scanner's lifetime use, time by temperature and factory elevation range, the USB drive use, and the largest distance from the first fix. A recording with all fixes within 50 m is noted as made on the ground. `ppk.pcmp` adds its range and rotation filters and the antenna offsets. With both files loaded, a check compares the recordings in `service.log` with the `.data` files the project processes.
- Wingtra LiDAR recordings `WINGTRA-<date>-<time>-NNN.data` are read: the 1 KiB header of the first part (payload, firmware, NovAtel receiver, Hesai scanner), the data streams with their rates (IMU 200 Hz, NovAtel GNSS, scanner packets), the time span from the IMU time stamps, and the positions, solution types and raw measurements (RANGECMP4) of the NovAtel messages inside. The recording is cut into 256 MiB parts regardless of records; a further part is recognised by its name, and a check confirms that it begins with exactly the rest of the record cut at the end of the previous part. The checksum that `service.log` gives for each part is not CRC-32, CRC-32C, Adler-32 or a byte sum and is not checked.
- NovAtel OEM binary: positions from `BESTPOS` (ellipsoidal height = height above mean sea level + undulation, σ, solution type) and the time of raw measurements (RANGE, RANGECMP, RANGECMP4) are now read; before, the messages were only counted.
- Wingtra payload computer (OBC) logs `obclogs/NNNNN_<date>_<time>.txt` (WingtraPilot 2.19–2.20) and the older `.xml` (2.8): firmware, Raspberry Pi hardware, camera, PPK triggers (GPS time, the same as the event time in the WingtraHub geotaglog) paired with the geotag items as exposures, flight lines, PPK receiver reports, message levels and the most frequent warnings and errors. The OBC clock is not set from GNSS; its offset is taken from the triggers (+3 h 12 min in the checked logs), and a log without triggers has no UTC time.
- Thermal TIFFs converted from DJI R-JPEGs by programs that do not name themselves (checked on internal M3T images converted by DJI Image Processor 1.7.5 by Miro Rava and by ThermoConverter 1.8 by VantageUAV, next to ThermoTIFF with the same parameters):
  - Both copy Software (the camera firmware) and ImageDescription (the palette name) from the R-JPEG and store no conversion parameters. They are now recognised as thermal (single band, DJI, infrared image source, palette name or `_T` file name), so the thermal fields of the specification apply.
  - The pixel values of single-band 16-bit and float32 TIFFs (uncompressed, Deflate or LZW strips, up to 64 Mpx) are read for their range and mean. Float32 values in the range of °C are reported as temperatures from an unnamed converter; DJI Image Processor agrees with ThermoTIFF to within 0.003 °C.
  - 16-bit values without a stated scale are reported as such. The ThermoConverter values fit (T + 50) × 1000 modulo 65 536, matching its settings "Minimum Temp −50" and "1000", so they overflow above 15.535 °C (49–89 % of the pixels of the checked images); a histogram with both ends full and the middle empty is flagged as overflow.
  - EXIF tags written into the GPS IFD (ThermoConverter puts CreateDate, FocalLength and BodySerialNumber there) are flagged. Missing drone-dji XMP after conversion (DJI Image Processor drops UTCAtExposure, ImageSource, AltitudeType and the rangefinder) is listed.
  - For ThermoTIFF TIFFs the temperature range of the pixels is shown as well.
- Thermal measurement parameters of DJI images are also checked against the limits of the DJI Thermal SDK: emissivity 0.10–1.00, relative humidity 20–100 % and reflected temperature −40 to 500 °C (as documented for the SDK wrapper in the theRmalUAV R package; the distance limit of 1–25 m was already checked).
- Export `footprints.geojson` (Batch and OpenSkyLab tabs): the ground outline of every image as a polygon, also for oblique images. Each corner is a ray from the camera (gimbal yaw, tilt from nadir and roll, sensor size and focal length) cut with flat ground at the take-off level, at the relative altitude or the height set by hand; images that see the horizon are left out and counted. Properties: image, time, height and its source, angles, GSD at the image centre, area. Checked against the nadir footprints of exposures.geojson (same corners).
- Checked on public data (Zenodo and MicaSense samples under CC BY 4.0 or MIT; see the data README of the project):
  - DJI Zenmuse H20T: the measurement parameters in APP4 (layout "ThermalParams") are now read: distance, humidity, emissivity ÷ 100 and reflected temperature ÷ 10; the scales follow from real images whose values equal the DJI Thermal SDK defaults (5 m, 70 %, 1.00, 23 °C). The text MakerNote `[sensor_id:…]` gives the sensor ID and is no longer read as an IFD (it raised an "implausible entry count" warning).
  - FLIR (Vue Pro R, XT2 on DJI) writes the sensor width in mm into FocalPlaneXResolution (10.88 = 640 × 17 µm). It is now read as such, so pixel pitch, field of view and GSD are right (before: 92 µm and 132°), and a warning says so.
  - Radiometric TIFFs from the camera: FLIR TLinear (XMP `Camera:TlinearGain`, kelvin = value × gain) and the MicaSense Altum / Altum-PT LWIR band (centikelvin) are recognised as thermal with their temperature range; FLIR TIFFs without TlinearGain are raw counts. A band named LWIR makes the sensor type thermal; the RGB image of Parrot Sequoia is RGB, not multispectral.
  - DJI Mavic 3M: `Camera:Irradiance` of the sunlight sensor has no stated unit and is no longer multiplied as if it were MicaSense DLS 1 (shown as 778 733 µW/cm²/nm before).
  - DNG (Mavic 3M): the sensor size uses the full-resolution raw image in the SubIFD, not the 160 px preview in IFD0.
  - When DateTimeOriginal − UTC is no real time-zone offset (Mavic 3M: +1:18:35), the reader no longer reports a zone such as +01:15; it shows the difference and warns that the camera clock was not set from GNSS.
  - TIFFs re-saved by ImageJ (Workswell WIRIS in a published set) are flagged: the camera's metadata are gone. ImageJ tags 50838/50839 have names.
  - CSV geotags: column names with underscores, `file`/`source`, `datetime_original` in EXIF format (read as camera clock), accuracy east/north/up, `accuracy_class`, absolute and relative altitude; `height` next to `width` is the image size, not the altitude. A CSV with one row and no image name or time is a single position (e.g. the base). A CSV with image names, GPS week and seconds of week gives exposure times without positions.
  - u-blox UBX without NAV-PVT (TOPODRONE PPK module, ZED-F9P): the track comes from NAV-POSLLH on the GPS week of RXM-RAWX; NAV-DOP and TIM-TP are named, and TIM-TM2 is counted under its name again.
  - Flight logs with modes, errors and vibration (ArduPilot, PX4, tlog) list them for the whole log, also without exposures: mode sequence, warnings and errors, largest vibration and clipping. ArduCopter 4.5 is verified.
- JPEG: DJI M3T R-JPEGs from 2026 declare a length of MPF image 1 that reaches a few hundred to a few thousand bytes past its EOI, with the preview right after; these bytes were reported as an unknown trailer and are now shown as part of MPF image 1.
- Czech view: version numbers with one dot (such as `v1.00`) are no longer written with a decimal comma.

## [1.4.0] - 2026-11-19

First release in this repository. Parser OSLMeta 1.0.0, flight log module OSLFlight 0.3.0, checks against the OpenSkyLab metadata specification v0.3.
Versions 1.2 and 1.3 were published on openskylab.space only, before the reader had its own repository; they are listed below so that the history is complete.

- Export of `provenance.json` next to `metadata.json`: the SHA-256 of every loaded image and log (computed in the browser), the reader and its component versions, the extraction activity with the SHA-256 of the `metadata.json` and `image_log.csv` it generated, the origin of every filled value (image tag, derivation, flight log; for log values the image's own value and whether it agrees) and, per column of `image_log.csv`, how many rows come from the image or the log. `metadata.json` names the record in a new top-level field `provenance` (draft for 0.4; the 0.3 schema accepts it).
- The draft `metadata.json` no longer carries values of the wrong type: empty slots only for free text ("") and lists ([]); numbers, enumerations and objects are left out until known; `oblique_images` is written as `{included, gimbal_pitch_deg}` as the schema requires; one empty product to fill in. Against the 0.3 schema the draft now fails only on the fields that must be filled after processing (product level and GSD).
- Flight logs: DJI FlightRecord (`DJIFlightRecord_*.txt` from DJI GO 4, DJI Pilot, Pix4Dcapture and other apps) read directly in the browser for format versions up to 12, following the layout in the open-source dji-log-parser: track, exposures (from the camera's remaining-photo counter) with time, position, height above take-off and gimbal angles, aircraft and camera serial numbers, app version, battery and satellites. Times come from the clock of the phone or tablet running the app; heights are relative to take-off, so they are not used as ellipsoid heights. Version 13 and later is encrypted with keys from the DJI API and is reported as such; the CSV written by dji-log-parser is still read as before.
- Flight logs: from an encrypted DJI FlightRecord v13+ the reader now reads the summary that is not AES-encrypted (the details in the first auxiliary block, XOR-scrambled like a record): aircraft name and serial number, camera serial number, start time and duration, maximum height, photo count, home point and app version; total distance only for the iOS and Android apps, whose unit was checked against decoded logs. Track and exposures still need the CSV from dji-log-parser.
- Derived values that rest on an assumption are marked as estimates ("≈ estimate" instead of "derived"): height above ground and distance taken as height above take-off, GSD, flight geometry and overlap, batch values from one image, GNSS mode and sensor type guessed from tags. The note next to the value names the assumption; `provenance.json` adds `"estimate": true`.
- Heights from a log are no longer called "agrees" or "differs" against the image when the image does not say its height is ellipsoidal (DJI AbsoluteAltitude, EXIF GPSAltitude): the reader shows the difference and "not compared, image height reference not documented", and `provenance.json` records `not_comparable` with the difference.
- Image–log matching is audited: every image gets a status (unique, ambiguous, conflict, unmatched), the method, the number of fitting records, the alternatives and the records rejected as too far (over 200 m or 120 s) with the reason. Two records that fit about equally (two flights with the same numbering and no time to tell them apart, exposures closer in time than the image clock resolution) are not resolved by guessing: the image stays without log values and a check says why. Two different shots matched to one exposure are reported as a conflict. The audit goes to `provenance.json` (`matching[]`, added to the 0.4 draft) and to the flight summary JSON. Synthetic tests cover renamed files, a missing and a duplicated image, two flights with the same numbering, band files of one multispectral shot, a camera clock offset, exposures closer than the clock resolution and a record too far away.
- A folder can be chosen ("Choose a folder…") or dropped: every image and log below it is read, and files that are neither (reports, PDFs, settings, hidden files) are left out with a note. With more than 20 files a line shows how many are read so far.
- Large batches: images are no longer read whole into memory. The reader keeps the File and the parser reads byte ranges (results identical on 40 real JPEG and TIFF files); SHA-256 is computed one file at a time; thumbnails point at the file. 1000 images (~10 GB) load in about 50 s with ~130–185 MB of page memory, where 300 images (3.1 GB) used to take 3.1 GB. The file list is redrawn at most about three times a second while loading.
- Flight logs, DJI: the dji-log-parser CSV drops rows whose date lies more than 12 h from the median (a decoded 2026 flight carried 2017 and 2043), so the flight span is no longer wrong; a generic aircraft name ("dji aircraft") gives way to the drone type; the total distance is converted from km for app platform 6 (Mavic 3 Enterprise series), where it is stored in km, and left out for platforms not checked; when the photo count is known but `CAMERA.isPhoto` is never set, the reader says that the exposures are not in the CSV. Product type 118 is named "Mavic 3 Enterprise series". FLY.DAT files marked `DJI_LOG_V3` with a build from 2023 on are reported as encrypted (older ones are only scrambled and stay "not decoded").
- Flight logs: senseFly eMotion console transcripts (`*_transcript_<n>.txt`: trigger commands as exposures with time and no position, the PC clock converted to UTC with the session header, take-off and landing, planned height, warnings) and the eBee X payload computer log (`log_<n>.txt`: firmware of each unit, flight session name, payloads and serial numbers, the thermal camera calibration written to XMP, and the moment the clock is set from GNSS; lines before that carry 1970 and are not used as times), and the Pix4Dcapture app log (app version, the drone and camera actually connected, the mission type opened and the overlaps and camera angle stored in the app, marked as app settings rather than the flown plan; local times converted to UTC with the start time in the file name).
- Flight logs: senseFly eBee / eBee Plus black box `.bbx` (self-describing variable log of eMotion 2): photo tags with UTC, position, WGS 84 height, horizontal and vertical accuracy and attitude, the u-blox track, identification and take-off altitude. eBee X `.bbz` flight archives (stored ZIP): the autopilot `.bb3` inside is read as before, together with the payload computer log, the build and the list of members; an archive still being written is read as far as it goes. Pix4Dcapture missions (`.pix4dcapture-mission`, `-plan`): mission type, overlaps, height above take-off, camera pitch, trigger, area, waypoints and the planned GSD fill the mission fields (planning software, pattern, nominal height, overlaps, gimbal pitch, nominal GSD) and are marked as the plan; the author's e-mail in the file is not read. DJI app flight events (`<date> <time>-<serial>.dat`): Home Point, RTH height, flight modes and warnings, in the phone's local time. DJI FlightRecord v8: shots from the camera shoot flag or from the remaining-photo counter, whichever matches the DJI capture count. DJI CSV from dji-log-parser: exposures from the rising edges of CAMERA.isPhoto (about ±1 s), double edges merged when that matches DETAILS.photoNum, with a note when shots are missing. DJI device log bundles (`*.lz.enc` members, e.g. Mavic 3M camera logs) are reported as encrypted. Recognised and described without decoding: DJI aircraft logs (`FLY*.DAT`, `*_MSDK.DAT`: firmware build), senseFly eMotion logbooks (`.efl`: list of variables) and Jeti transmitter telemetry (value range per channel).
- Processing projects: Pix4Dmapper `.p4d` and Agisoft Metashape (`doc.xml`, `chunk.zip`, `.psz`; a `.psx` is recognised as a pointer to its `.files` folder) are read in the browser. From the project: software and version, the processing steps with their settings (Metashape also duration and memory per step), image and aligned image counts, prior accuracy of image positions, ground control and check points, reference systems and accuracies, camera rig, adjusted camera calibration and the products. They fill `processing[]` (software, steps, photogrammetry settings, adjustment quality, output CRS), `products[]` (type, CRS, workflow), the GCP counts and, for Metashape, the adjusted calibration of a sensor matched by image size; the check table marks them as coming from the project. Image paths stored in a project are not reported.
- `provenance.json` with a processing project: the project as an input (`processing_project`), the processing software as an agent, and one `processing` activity per recorded step with its parameters, the loaded images the project lists and the products the step made.
- Weather from the Open-Meteo archive, only on request: the OpenSkyLab check offers "Add weather from Open-Meteo…", says what will be sent (the mean position of the flight rounded to 0.01°, about 1 km, and the date) and asks again before sending. The hourly values for the flight (temperature, humidity, pressure, wind speed and direction, cloud cover) fill the environment fields as estimates ("model value for a grid cell, not measured on site"); a value from the image or a field record takes precedence. `metadata.json` gets `missions[].environment.weather_source` and `sampling_interval`, `provenance.json` records the values with origin `external_service` (added to the 0.4 draft), and `environment.csv` (the template's columns, with the sun position per hour) can be downloaded. Without UTC time the reader says that a flight log or a time zone is needed.
- A Content-Security-Policy in the page lets the browser block every request it does not declare. The standalone files allow only the weather archive; the website page also allows its own files and the visit statistics. The fonts of the standalone files are embedded (SIL OFL 1.1, `LICENSES/`) instead of loaded from Google Fonts, so opening the page contacts no other server. The privacy note names the weather request as the one exception.
- A headline under the title: "Check your photos and flight logs together. Traceable matching, a metadata check and a report — with no data upload."

## [1.3] - 2026-11-02

Website only (openskylab.space/reader), no tag.

- DJI `RtkFlag` follows the DJI OSDK values: 50 fixed (narrow-lane integer), 48–49 lower-grade integer, 32–34 float, 16 single point, 0 none.
- Checked against field data from DJI (M3M, M3T, Mavic 2 Pro, Phantom 4 Pro, Zenmuse X5S), MicaSense (RedEdge-M, RedEdge-P), Parrot Sequoia, senseFly (thermoMAP, Duet T, S.O.D.A., Aeria X), Sony on WingtraOne, Workswell WIRIS, MAPIR and Tetracam. Fixes and additions:
  - DJI writes GPS time, not UTC, to `UTCAtExposure` and `GPSTimeStamp`; UTC now subtracts the leap seconds (18 s since 2017). New derived value: camera clock offset.
  - DJI `RtkFlag` 0 with a valid position (drones without RTK) is reported as single point, not as no solution.
  - MicaSense DLS2 irradiance is no longer multiplied by 100 (`Camera:Irradiance` is already in µW/cm²/nm).
  - XMP rational strings such as `30/10` are read as numbers (WIRIS GNSS accuracy).
  - senseFly XMP namespace: attitude and GNSS accuracy; senseFly and Parrot write EXIF times in UTC; senseFly `GPSAltitude` is ellipsoidal height.
  - Parrot Sequoia: sunshine sensor records (`IrradianceList`) decoded, calibration pictures flagged.
  - Warnings for a camera clock that was not set and for invalid sensor temperatures; MAPIR Survey3 bands from the filter code; a JPEG after EOI is found even when padded.
- New Flight tab (`src/metadata-reader_flight.js`, OSLFlight 0.2.0): reads flight, GNSS and geotag logs in the browser and joins them with the images by file name, DJI MRK index, MicaSense CaptureId or time.
  - Formats: DJI Timestamp.MRK, RINEX 2/3, RTCM 3, u-blox UBX, PX4 ULog (also the Wingtra variant), Septentrio SBF, senseFly eBee X `.bb3` (the GNSS receiver stream inside it), senseFly geoinfo, eMotion KML, Trimble JobXML, Wingtra geotaglog, json, ini and geotags.csv, MicaSense paramlog and gpslog, Parrot Sequoia TLMB, Wingtra LiDAR service.log and ppk.pcmp. NovAtel OEM binary and encrypted logs are recognised and reported.
  - Checks: exposure counts, images joined, camera clock against GNSS, image position against the log, solution of the exposures, raw rover data for PPK, base coverage of the flight, opposite flight lines, calibration panel, sun elevation, C2PA manifests.
  - Exports: exposures CSV and a flight summary JSON. Large logs are read in blocks.
  - Version check: a table of the format and firmware versions each parser was checked on. A log from another version is read the same way and flagged so that its values are checked (a warning for proprietary formats, a note for published standards).
  - Plausibility: positions outside the valid range, heights outside −500 to 12 000 m, times before 1980 or in the future and negative or huge σ are removed and counted; positions 0, 0 are treated as no fix. Jumps above 120 m/s between exposures are counted.
  - Files that look like logs but are not recognised, and recognised files with nothing readable, are listed in the checks.
  - Generic formats: ArduPilot DataFlash `.bin` and text `.log` (fields found by name through the FMT messages, so older and newer column sets both work), MAVLink `.tlog` (GPS_RAW_INT, CAMERA_FEEDBACK, CAMERA_TRIGGER), NMEA 0183 (GGA, RMC, ZDA, GST), GPX and CSV geotags (Pix4D, Metashape, Emlid and similar; columns found by name, delimiter detected, projected coordinates recognised but not converted).
  - KML placemarks count as exposures only when they are named like image files; mission waypoints are listed separately.
  - RINEX in Compact RINEX (Hatanaka) form and gzip-compressed files (`.crx.gz`, `.rnx.gz`, as distributed by IGS, EUREF and national networks) are read directly; epoch times are rebuilt, observations are not decoded.
  - Base stations: consecutive files of one station (for example 15-minute 1 s files) are joined before the coverage check; the distance from the base to the flight is reported (ideally within 10 km for PPK, a warning beyond 30 km).
  - DJI FlightRecord: reads the CSV written by the open-source decoder dji-log-parser (`dji-log --csv`); FlightRecord v13+ is encrypted and its keys come from the DJI API with the user's own App Key, so decoding stays outside the browser.
  - A damaged message in a ULog or a damaged block in a Sequoia TLMB file is skipped and counted; the rest of the log is still read.
  - The tool build (`metadata-reader_tool.html`) is English only and has no synthetic samples; the teaching build keeps Czech and English and the samples.
  - Checked locally on public logs (not included): ArduPlane 4.4, ArduCopter 4.3 and 4.6, PX4 1.16, DJI Mavic 3E Timestamp.MRK with images.
  - Tests on synthetic logs built from the documented formats (`test/flight_test.js`); checked locally against field data and public PX4 and RTKLIB sample logs.

- The page is organised as three steps: images, flight log (optional) and the OpenSkyLab check; each step shows where it stands. All tags and Batch are secondary views. Images and logs have separate boxes; files are still recognised by content.
- The OpenSkyLab check uses the flight logs: for a matched image the exposure time, position, RTK state and σ (height only when the log gives it above the ellipsoid) come from the log, with the image's own value shown next to it as agreeing or differing; mission fields (flight span, number of exposures, GNSS mode, time offset, correction source, log reference) are filled from the logs. Without a log the reader works from the image data and says what a log would add.
- Flight geometry from the exposure positions in the log, or from the image positions without a log: flight lines, exposure and line spacing, flight pattern, forward and side overlap, and motion blur (speed × shutter time / GSD). The values fill the mission fields and appear as checks.
- Exports: camera positions as CSV for Metashape and Pix4D, geo.txt for OpenDroneMap, and a flight quality report (a standalone HTML page to print or save as PDF) with a short explanation for each check. metadata.json and image_log.csv include the values from the logs.
- SfM and GIS exports ready to import: Metashape reference CSV (position, yaw/pitch/roll, accuracy per axis), PIX4Dmatic image geolocation (no header, latitude/longitude order, angles and accuracy), OpenDroneMap geo.txt, and GeoJSON for QGIS (exposure points with solution state and σ, footprints of nadir images). Camera angles come from the DJI gimbal converted to the SfM convention (yaw from north to the image top, pitch 0 = nadir); a nadir shot that DJI writes as roll 180° is turned back to the equivalent rotation with roll 0.
